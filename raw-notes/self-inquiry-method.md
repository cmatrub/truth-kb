# Self-inquiry method
The goal is to disengage from external objects (the world) and just be the Self.

To do this, simply turn the attention to yourself. Don't worry about whether the mind has disengaged from the non-self. Turning the attention to the Self automatically disengages it from everything else.

And that's it. There is nothing more to do.

Self-inquiry is not an action. It is being silent, abiding as one's own being. So once the attention is turned, there is nothing further to do.

## Comparison with yoga
Yoga has the same goal: *yogas chitta vritti nirodhah*, the stilling of the mind's movements (Yoga Sutra 1.2). But the yogic method tries to keep thoughts away. That makes it much harder, and it won't lead to the annihilation of the ego.

## Analogy: the monkey and the elephant
A doctor told a patient not to think of a monkey while taking his medicine. The patient couldn't do it. Every time he took the medicine, he had to remind himself not to think of the monkey, which meant thinking of the monkey.

A wiser doctor told the patient to think of an elephant instead. That was easy.

Self-inquiry works like the second doctor's advice. Instead of fighting thoughts, you give the attention something to rest on: the Self.
